AeroE Checklist – User guide

AeroE Checklist is a tap-to-tick electronic checklist for common general aviation aircraft. It runs on a phone or tablet, works without a network once installed, and keeps your place in each checklist on the device.

Prototype content. Every checklist was drafted from typical manufacturer procedures, not from an approved document. Verify each type against the operator's approved checklist and the aircraft's POH/AFM before any use. The footer notice stays until that has been done.

1. Installing on a phone or tablet

The app is a web page that installs like an app. You need signal once, for the install.

  1. Open https://swalksa350.github.io/AeroE-checklist/ in Safari (iPhone, iPad) or Chrome (Android).
  2. Tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  3. Open it once from the new icon while still online. From then on it launches full-screen and works with no network.

Anyone with the link can install it the same way. Each device keeps its own progress and favourites; nothing is shared between devices.

2. Choosing an aircraft and favourites

The app opens on the aircraft picker, grouped into single-engine piston, high-performance single, multi-engine piston and turboprop. Each card shows the maker, model, engine and whether any checklist is under way on that type. Tap a card to open it.

  • Favourites. Tap the ☆ in the corner of a card to pin that aircraft under a Favourites heading at the top of the picker. Tap the ★ to remove it. The same choice is available on the aircraft's Status page.
  • Suggested favourites. After you have opened an aircraft three times the app offers to add it. "Not now" silences the offer for another ten opens. Once you have added or removed an aircraft yourself it never asks about that type again.
  • Continue. The picker offers the aircraft you used last at the top.
  • Changing aircraft. Tap the aircraft name at the top left of any screen, or use "Change aircraft" on the Status page.

3. The screen and the four tabs

Once an aircraft is open, the header shows its short name on the left, four tabs, and the display switch on the right. On a phone you see a list of checklists first and tap one to open it; on a tablet the list stays on the left with the open checklist on the right.

NormalThe flight sequence: Before Start, Engine Start, Before Taxi, Before Takeoff, After Takeoff / Climb, Cruise, Descent, Before Landing, After Landing, Shutdown. The first entry, V-speeds & limits, is a reference page, not a checklist. AbnormalCaution-level problems: alternator failure, low oil pressure, gear unsafe, icing and so on. Action is needed but not immediately. EmergencyWarning-level procedures: engine failure, fire, emergency descent, decompression. Immediate action. ChartsLinks that open the Airservices DAP, ERSA and NAIPS pages, or your chart app, outside AeroE. See section 12. StatusWhere you are across the whole flight: procedures in progress, items deferred to landing, normal checklist completion, favourites, customised checklists, EFB files, voice settings, version and updates. A number on the tab means an abnormal or emergency procedure is still open.

The colours follow cockpit convention: green for normal, amber for caution, red for warning. A dot beside each checklist in the list shows its state: grey not started, amber part done, green complete, red for an abnormal or emergency procedure still in progress.

4. Working through a checklist

Each line is a challenge on the left and a response on the right, the way a paper checklist reads. The line to action next has a coloured bar on its left edge and a lighter background.

  • Tick an item by tapping anywhere on the line. It greys out and gains a tick. Tap again to untick.
  • Branches. Some procedures ask a question in amber, for example "Engine condition?" with Cold, Hot and Flooded. Tap the answer and only the lines that apply appear. Changing the answer clears ticks under the old one.
  • Notes in red are warnings and in amber are cautions. They are read, not ticked.
  • Complete. When every item is ticked a green bar appears. In the normal sequence it offers the next checklist; in a procedure it offers the Status page.
  • Reset at the top right clears that one checklist. Resetting everything for a new flight is on the Status page.

5. Abnormal and emergency procedures

  • Memory items have a red bar on the left and a red "(memory)" tag. These are the recall actions to do first; the app is for confirming them afterwards.
  • Land banners. A red banner reads LAND IMMEDIATELY or LAND AS SOON AS POSSIBLE; an amber one reads LAND AS SOON AS PRACTICAL. They follow the usual meanings of those phrases.
  • Entry condition. Some procedures show the triggering indication under the title, for example the annunciator that brings you here.
  • Deferred items appear in amber with the response NOTED. Ticking one records a consequence for later, such as "flaps inoperative – flaps-up landing, longer runway". They are collected on the Status page under landing considerations.
  • In emergency procedures the responses are white rather than cyan so that red stays the only accent on the page.

6. The Status page

  • Active procedures lists any abnormal or emergency procedure started and not finished, with its progress and land banner. Tap one to return to it.
  • Deferred items / landing considerations gathers every deferred item you have ticked.
  • Normal checklists shows completion of the flight sequence at a glance.
  • Flight holds "Reset all … for a new flight", which asks you to confirm before clearing that aircraft only, plus the favourite toggle and Change aircraft.
  • EFB files for this aircraft is covered in section 10.
  • Customised checklists lists the approach checklist and any edited normal checklists, the shared-approach switch, and Export / Import. See section 11.
  • Voice holds every voice setting, the microphone test and the voice log. See section 13.
  • App shows the version number, a What's new row, the user guide link, when updates were last checked and a manual check button. The build code is in small print below.

7. Day and night display

The switch at the far right of the header cycles through three settings:

  • Auto follows the phone's own light or dark setting.
  • Day forces the light display: pale grey background with darker colours for glare.
  • Night forces the dark display: charcoal background, softened text and desaturated colours for a dark cockpit.

The choice is remembered on the device and applied before the page draws, so there is no flash on launch. The hues never change between modes, only brightness and background.

8. Updates and what's new

Updates arrive automatically; nothing has to be reinstalled.

  • Opening the app with signal loads the newest version. The first launch after an update shows a green bar naming the version and what changed, with a What's new button for the full notes.
  • If a new version finishes downloading while the app is open, an amber bar appears with the incoming version's headline and a Refresh now button.
  • Release notes for every version are under What's new on the Status page.
  • After a new version is published it can take up to ten minutes to reach every device, because the web host keeps the previous files for that long. "Check for updates now" on the Status page picks it up once it is there.

9. Offline use and saved progress

  • After the first online visit every part of the app is stored on the device, including all aircraft. No network is needed to open it or use any checklist.
  • Ticks, branch answers, favourites, the display setting and the aircraft you were on are saved on the device as you go. They survive closing the app, locking the phone and updates.
  • Progress is kept separately for each aircraft. Resetting one does not affect another.
  • The screen is kept awake while a checklist is open, on devices that allow it.

10. EFB files for OzRunways and AvPlan

For pilots who prefer to keep everything inside their EFB, each aircraft is also available as two stand-alone files. Find them on the aircraft's Status page under "EFB files for this aircraft", or all together from the link at the bottom of the picker.

  • Interactive checklist (HTML). The same tap-to-tick checklist in one file that works offline inside the EFB's document viewer. On an iPad or iPhone, open the link, tap Share, then Copy to OzRWY; it appears on the OzRunways Documents page. Or save the file to the Dropbox folder linked to your EFB. Companies can push the file to every pilot through the OzRunways Dashboard or an AvPlan Cloud Group.
  • Printable checklist (PDF). An A4 copy of every procedure with branches shown in full, for AvPlan (which documents PDF import) or for paper.
The EFB files carry the content of the version they were downloaded from and do not update themselves. After an app update, download the file again and replace it in the EFB. Ticks inside the EFB viewer are kept between sessions only where that viewer allows browser storage.

11. Direct links to an aircraft

Adding # and the aircraft's id to the app address opens that aircraft straight away, for example …/AeroE-checklist/#c172s. The id is shown in the address bar when an aircraft is open. Useful for a club or school that flies one type.

12. Charts tab

Charts cannot be displayed inside AeroE: Jeppesen FD Pro is a separate app, and the Airservices AIP site does not allow its pages to be shown inside another site. The Charts tab instead gives one-tap links that open outside the app: the DAP index, ERSA, NAIPS, an AIP search for the aerodrome code you enter, and buttons that switch to FD Pro, OzRunways or AvPlan when they are installed. The practical arrangement on an iPad is Split View: with AeroE open, swipe up slightly to show the Dock and drag your chart app or Safari to the side of the screen. Both stay visible through departure and approach. ERSA is not a separate app: it is inside OzRunways and AvPlan, so the Open OzRunways button is the quickest route to an aerodrome's entry. The NAIPS app publishes no link name, so “Try NAIPS app” may not work on every device; the website link always does. The Charts page needs signal and is not stored offline.

In Split View the header shortens its tab labels to fit the narrower window.

13. Voice: read aloud, spoken replies and recall

Voice is off until you turn it on under Status → Voice. It has three parts.

Read aloud

With Read aloud on, opening any checklist shows a voice bar at the bottom with a Start button. The app speaks each line, challenge then response, highlights it, and waits. Tap ✓ Checked (or the line itself) to tick it and hear the next. Repeat says it again, Skip moves on without ticking, Stop pauses. Notes are read and passed over; at a branch the app reads the question and the options and waits for you to choose. In normal checklists it reads the short form by default: the challenge and the capitalised action, for example “Fuel shutoff, ON”, leaving bracketed detail on the screen; abnormal and emergency procedures are always read in full, and the short form can be switched off under Status → Voice. At the end it says the checklist is complete. Speaking rate and voice are set on the Status page, and Test the voice lets you hear them. Read aloud works offline and plays through a paired Bluetooth headset.

Voice replies (experimental)

With Voice replies on, the app listens for a few seconds after each line. Say “checked”, or the response as written in capitals (for example “both on” or “idle cutoff”), to tick and continue; “repeat”, “skip” or “stop”. At a branch, say the first word of the option, for example “hot” or “no”. The bar shows a red dot while listening and prints what it heard, so a wrong hearing is visible before it matters; a tick can always be undone by tapping the line. The first use asks for microphone permission. On iPad and iPhone the recognition is done by Apple and needs signal; Android can work offline. A paired Bluetooth headset supplies the microphone, which keeps engine noise out. If the app cannot hear you it simply waits for a tap.

Say procedure

With Voice replies on, a Say procedure button appears above the checklist list. Tap it and say the name, for example “landing checklist”, “engine failure in flight” or “low oil pressure”. The app opens the best match for the current aircraft and, if Read aloud is on, starts reading it.

“Hey Aero” wake word

Turn on “Hey Aero” wake word under Status → Voice and the app keeps the microphone open while it is on screen. Say “hey Aero, landing checklist”, “hey Aero, before takeoff”, “hey Aero, engine failure in flight”, “hey Aero, approach checklist”, “hey Aero, status” or “hey Aero, stop”. A single tone acknowledges the wake word if you pause after it; two tones confirm the command. A red dot beside the aircraft name shows the app is listening. It only works while the app is in front: an installed web app cannot listen in the background or with the screen locked. It uses more battery and, on iPad and iPhone, needs signal.

Editing normal checklists

Every normal checklist can be tailored to an operator's requirements. Open it and tap Edit at the top right: add, change, remove or reorder lines, add a note, or use Reload original lines to start again. Branch questions such as “Engine condition?” stay as a block that can be moved or deleted but not edited inside. Write the action in capitals so the voice reads it in short form. Save, and the list shows ✎ beside the name and “Customised on this device” under the title; Restore original removes all edits. Abnormal and emergency procedures cannot be edited.

Edits are kept on this device, per aircraft. To give a fleet the same checklists, make the edits on one device, use Export customisations on the Status page and send the text to each pilot, who opens the same aircraft and uses Import to paste it. Importing replaces that device's edits for that aircraft.

Custom approach checklist

Each aircraft can also have one checklist of your own. On the Status page, under Customised checklists, tap Create next to the approach entry to start from a template or a blank list. Save, and it appears under Normal between Descent and Before Landing. It is included in Export and Import.

One approach checklist for all favourites. Turn this switch on, in the same section, and every aircraft you have marked ★ uses a single shared approach checklist; editing it from any favourite changes it for all of them. Aircraft that are not favourites keep their own. If the aircraft you are on already had its own approach checklist when you turn the switch on, that list becomes the shared one.

Voice settings on the Status page

  • Read aloud, Short responses in normal checklists (challenge plus the capitalised action only; abnormal and emergency are always read in full), “Hey Aero” wake word, Cue tones (one tone when listening starts, two when a word is accepted), and Voice replies.
  • Open microphone early appears on Android only. On iPad, iPhone and Mac speech and listening cannot overlap, so the microphone opens as soon as a line has been read: wait for the single tone, then reply.
  • Speaking rate in five steps, each played as a sample when tapped, and a choice of Voice from those on the device.
  • Test the voice speaks a sample line. Test the microphone listens once and prints exactly what the device reported, including any error code.
  • Under the wake word switch a status line shows whether the wake listener is running and the last words it heard. The Voice log at the end records recent voice events with times, for troubleshooting.
  • Replies are accepted as “checked” or the written response itself (“both on”, “idle cutoff”), plus “repeat”, “skip” and “stop”. A phrase containing “hey Aero” is never treated as a reply.
Voice is an aid to the displayed checklist, not a replacement for it. Read the screen, and treat a spoken tick like any other tick.

14. If something looks wrong

  • A change you were told about is not showing. Check the Version row in the App section of the Status page. If it is older than expected, wait ten minutes from publication, tap "Check for updates now", close the app fully and reopen it. The green bar on the first launch after an update names the version and has a What's new button.
  • A voice-opened checklist is not read aloud. Tap anywhere in the app once after launching it; Apple requires a tap before the app may speak. Then check the Voice log for “speech started” after each “speak:” line.
  • The wake word opens the wrong checklist. Say the full name and pause briefly after it. The voice log shows what was heard and what was opened.
  • There is no Version row. The copy on the device is from before updates were tracked, usually one installed from the old address. Delete the icon and install again from the address in section 1. Progress and favourites carry over.
  • Progress disappeared. Browser site data was cleared, or the app was opened in a private window. Saved data lives only in the device's browser storage.
  • Voice replies do nothing. Check that the microphone was allowed for the app (Settings → the browser or the installed app → Microphone), that the device has signal on iPad or iPhone, and that Voice replies is on under Status → Voice. If the device still cannot hear you, Read aloud with a tap to confirm continues to work.
  • A figure or procedure looks wrong. It may well be. The repository's review list records which figures the authors were least sure of for each aircraft, and corrections are made in the data file for that type. Report it with the aircraft and the checklist line.
AeroE Checklist is an open prototype. Content and software are provided as a starting point for an operator's own approved checklist, not as a substitute for one.
