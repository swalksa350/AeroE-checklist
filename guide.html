AeroE Checklist – User guide

AeroE Checklist is a tap-to-tick electronic checklist for common general aviation aircraft. It runs on a phone or tablet, works without a network once installed, and keeps your place in each checklist on the device.

Prototype content. Every checklist was drafted from typical manufacturer procedures, not from an approved document. Verify each type against the operator's approved checklist and the aircraft's POH/AFM before any use. The footer notice stays until that has been done.

1. Installing on a phone or tablet

The app is a web page that installs like an app. You need signal once, for the install.

  1. Open https://swalksa350.github.io/AeroE-checklist/ in Safari (iPhone, iPad) or Chrome (Android).
  2. Tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  3. Open it once from the new icon while still online. From then on it launches full-screen and works with no network.

Anyone with the link can install it the same way. Each device keeps its own progress and favourites; nothing is shared between devices.

2. Choosing an aircraft and favourites

The app opens on the aircraft picker, grouped into single-engine piston, high-performance single, multi-engine piston and turboprop. Each card shows the maker, model, engine and whether any checklist is under way on that type. Tap a card to open it.

  • Favourites. Tap the ☆ in the corner of a card to pin that aircraft under a Favourites heading at the top of the picker. Tap the ★ to remove it. The same choice is available on the aircraft's Status page.
  • Suggested favourites. After you have opened an aircraft three times the app offers to add it. "Not now" silences the offer for another ten opens. Once you have added or removed an aircraft yourself it never asks about that type again.
  • Continue. The picker offers the aircraft you used last at the top.
  • Changing aircraft. Tap the aircraft name at the top left of any screen, or use "Change aircraft" on the Status page.

3. The screen and the four tabs

Once an aircraft is open, the header shows its short name on the left, four tabs, and the display switch on the right. On a phone you see a list of checklists first and tap one to open it; on a tablet the list stays on the left with the open checklist on the right.

NormalThe flight sequence: Before Start, Engine Start, Before Taxi, Before Takeoff, After Takeoff / Climb, Cruise, Descent, Before Landing, After Landing, Shutdown. The first entry, V-speeds & limits, is a reference page, not a checklist. AbnormalCaution-level problems: alternator failure, low oil pressure, gear unsafe, icing and so on. Action is needed but not immediately. EmergencyWarning-level procedures: engine failure, fire, emergency descent, decompression. Immediate action. W&BWeight and balance for a specific registration of the current type. See section 12a. PerfTake-off and landing distances and speeds from the POH charts. See section 12b. PlanNAIPS briefing, requirements, fuel plan and flight plan filing through the operator connector. See section 12d. ChartsLinks that open the Airservices DAP, ERSA and NAIPS pages, or your chart app, outside AeroE. See section 12. StatusWhere you are across the whole flight: procedures in progress, items deferred to landing, normal checklist completion, favourites, customised checklists, EFB files, voice settings, version and updates. A number on the tab means an abnormal or emergency procedure is still open.

The colours follow cockpit convention: green for normal, amber for caution, red for warning. A dot beside each checklist in the list shows its state: grey not started, amber part done, green complete, red for an abnormal or emergency procedure still in progress.

4. Working through a checklist

Each line is a challenge on the left and a response on the right, the way a paper checklist reads. The line to action next has a coloured bar on its left edge and a lighter background.

  • Tick an item by tapping anywhere on the line. It greys out and gains a tick. Tap again to untick.
  • Branches. Some procedures ask a question in amber, for example "Engine condition?" with Cold, Hot and Flooded. Tap the answer and only the lines that apply appear. Changing the answer clears ticks under the old one.
  • Notes in red are warnings and in amber are cautions. They are read, not ticked.
  • Complete. When every item is ticked a green bar appears. In the normal sequence it offers the next checklist; in a procedure it offers the Status page.
  • Reset at the top right clears that one checklist. Resetting everything for a new flight is on the Status page.

5. Abnormal and emergency procedures

  • Memory items have a red bar on the left and a red "(memory)" tag. These are the recall actions to do first; the app is for confirming them afterwards.
  • Land banners. A red banner reads LAND IMMEDIATELY or LAND AS SOON AS POSSIBLE; an amber one reads LAND AS SOON AS PRACTICAL. They follow the usual meanings of those phrases.
  • Entry condition. Some procedures show the triggering indication under the title, for example the annunciator that brings you here.
  • Deferred items appear in amber with the response NOTED. Ticking one records a consequence for later, such as "flaps inoperative – flaps-up landing, longer runway". They are collected on the Status page under landing considerations.
  • In emergency procedures the responses are white rather than cyan so that red stays the only accent on the page.

6. The Status page

  • Active procedures lists any abnormal or emergency procedure started and not finished, with its progress and land banner. Tap one to return to it.
  • Deferred items / landing considerations gathers every deferred item you have ticked.
  • Normal checklists shows completion of the flight sequence at a glance.
  • Flight holds "Reset all … for a new flight", which asks you to confirm before clearing that aircraft only, plus the favourite toggle and Change aircraft.
  • EFB files for this aircraft is covered in section 10.
  • Customised checklists lists the approach checklist and any edited normal checklists, the shared-approach switch, and Export / Import. See section 11.
  • Voice holds every voice setting, the microphone test and the voice log. See section 13.
  • App shows the version number, a What's new row, the user guide link, when updates were last checked and a manual check button. The build code is in small print below.

7. Day and night display

The switch at the far right of the header cycles through three settings:

  • Auto follows the phone's own light or dark setting.
  • Day forces the light display: pale grey background with darker colours for glare.
  • Night forces the dark display: charcoal background, softened text and desaturated colours for a dark cockpit.

The choice is remembered on the device and applied before the page draws, so there is no flash on launch. The hues never change between modes, only brightness and background.

8. Updates and what's new

Updates arrive automatically; nothing has to be reinstalled.

  • Opening the app with signal loads the newest version. The first launch after an update shows a green bar naming the version and what changed, with a What's new button for the full notes.
  • If a new version finishes downloading while the app is open, an amber bar appears with the incoming version's headline and a Refresh now button.
  • Release notes for every version are under What's new on the Status page.
  • After a new version is published it can take up to ten minutes to reach every device, because the web host keeps the previous files for that long. "Check for updates now" on the Status page picks it up once it is there.

9. Offline use and saved progress

  • After the first online visit every part of the app is stored on the device, including all aircraft. No network is needed to open it or use any checklist.
  • Ticks, branch answers, favourites, the display setting and the aircraft you were on are saved on the device as you go. They survive closing the app, locking the phone and updates.
  • Progress is kept separately for each aircraft. Resetting one does not affect another.
  • The screen is kept awake while a checklist is open, on devices that allow it.

10. EFB files for OzRunways and AvPlan

For pilots who prefer to keep everything inside their EFB, each aircraft is also available as two stand-alone files. Find them on the aircraft's Status page under "EFB files for this aircraft", or all together from the link at the bottom of the picker.

  • Interactive checklist (HTML). The same tap-to-tick checklist in one file that works offline inside the EFB's document viewer. On an iPad or iPhone, open the link, tap Share, then Copy to OzRWY; it appears on the OzRunways Documents page. Or save the file to the Dropbox folder linked to your EFB. Companies can push the file to every pilot through the OzRunways Dashboard or an AvPlan Cloud Group.
  • Printable checklist (PDF). An A4 copy of every procedure with branches shown in full, for AvPlan (which documents PDF import) or for paper.
The EFB files carry the content of the version they were downloaded from and do not update themselves. After an app update, download the file again and replace it in the EFB. Ticks inside the EFB viewer are kept between sessions only where that viewer allows browser storage.

Full app file. Status › EFB files › Full app file builds, on the device, a single HTML file containing the whole app for the current aircraft (checklists, W&B, performance, planning and records) for OzRunways or AvPlan documents. It keeps its own data on the device it runs on and does not update itself, so rebuild it after an app update. Briefings and filing inside it still need the operator connector.

11. Direct links to an aircraft

Adding # and the aircraft's id to the app address opens that aircraft straight away, for example …/AeroE-checklist/#c172s. The id is shown in the address bar when an aircraft is open. Useful for a club or school that flies one type.

12. Charts tab

Charts cannot be displayed inside AeroE: Jeppesen FD Pro is a separate app, and the Airservices AIP site does not allow its pages to be shown inside another site. The Charts tab instead gives one-tap links that open outside the app: the AIP index, the DAP index and ERSA contents for the current amendment cycle, an AIP search for the aerodrome code you enter, the NAIPS app or website, and buttons that switch to FliteDeck Pro X, OzRunways or AvPlan when they are installed. The practical arrangement on an iPad is Split View: with AeroE open, swipe up slightly to show the Dock and drag your chart app or Safari to the side of the screen. Both stay visible through departure and approach. ERSA is not a separate app: it is inside OzRunways and AvPlan, so the Open OzRunways button is the quickest route to an aerodrome's entry. Open NAIPS app switches to the NAIPS app when it is installed; the website link is beside it. The Charts page needs signal and is not stored offline.

In Split View the header shortens its tab labels to fit the narrower window.

12a. Weight and balance

The W&B tab computes weight and balance for one specific airframe. It is available for every type, with type templates for the C208B and GA8 that pre-fill the station layout, arms, units, fuel density, limits and CG envelope; other types start blank. The GA8 template is taken from the GA8 manual GA-FM-04 (firewall datum, kilograms and millimetres) and lists both the seat rows and the freighter cargo zones, so load whichever the aircraft is configured with. The main cargo area limit of 680 kg across zones 1A to 1C is not totalled for you.

The GA8 comes with an example registration, VH-WSM, so you can try the tab at once. It is marked TEST DATA because its empty weight and arm are fictitious (the manual's worked example), not from any aircraft's weight and balance report. To remove it, open it, tap Edit profile, then Delete registration twice; it stays deleted.
  1. Add a registration. Tap + Add registration, enter the registration, then complete the profile: basic empty weight and arm from that aircraft's current weight and balance report (and its date), the station arms and any station limits, fuel arm, density and usable capacity, the maximum weights, and the CG envelope as forward and aft points of weight and arm from the POH. Every figure copied from a template is marked unverified until you have checked it.
  2. Load the flight. Enter the weight at each station, fuel on board, taxi fuel and trip burn in the profile's units. Results update as you type: zero-fuel, ramp, take-off and landing weights with their CG arms and the forward and aft limits at that weight, the fuel weights, any station or weight limit exceeded, and a WITHIN LIMITS or OUTSIDE LIMITS verdict. The envelope plot shows the three points with the take-off to landing line.
  3. Fuel. The three fuel rows (fuel on board, taxi, trip burn) each show a volume box on the left, in litres or US gallons as you choose from the small selector, and a weight box on the right in the profile's weight unit. Type either one and the other follows through the profile's fuel density. The fuel plan, performance and records read the volume in the aircraft's own unit.
  4. Keep it. Loads are remembered per registration, so a regular flight is a few taps. Clear loads resets them. Profiles are included in Export and Import, so a school can issue its fleet's registrations.

Manifest

Above the loads is the flight manifest: date, departure and destination, pilot in command and a flight or booking reference, then Crew and passengers (name, role, seat and weight) and Baggage and freight (description, location and weight). Each person or item is assigned to one of the registration's stations, and while Use the manifest for the station loads is ticked the Loads table is filled from the manifest totals and shown read-only. Untick it to type loads directly. Weights are entered as actual weights or the standard weights your operation is approved to use. Clear manifest removes the people and items but keeps the pilot's name.

Flight records

Save flight record (at the bottom of the W&B page; Preview shows it first) stores a dated snapshot of the manifest, the weight and balance calculation with every item's weight, arm and moment, the zero-fuel, ramp, take-off and landing conditions against their limits, and the Perf tab's take-off and landing inputs and results for the current registration. Records are listed in the W&B side panel under Flight records. Open one to:

  • Share PDF – builds a PDF on the device (no signal needed) and opens the share sheet, so it can be saved to Files, sent by mail or dropped into the operator's records system. On a computer it downloads.
  • Print – prints the record (or Save as PDF from the print dialog).
  • Copy text – copies a plain-text version.
  • Delete record – two taps; records are otherwise kept on the device, the newest 100 per type.

Digital sign-off

Each record is accepted twice: the pre-flight acceptance (manifest, W&B, performance and plan) before departure, and the completion acceptance (times, landings, approach, fuel and fuel checks) after the flight. Each has its own fingerprint, computed over the fields it covers, so completing the flight does not disturb the pre-flight acceptance. Each saved record has a Sign-off box. The pilot in command enters their name (pre-filled from the manifest) and optionally a licence or ARN number, ticks the acceptance statement and taps Accept and sign. The app stores the name, the time of acceptance and a SHA-256 fingerprint of the record as it stood when accepted. Whenever the record is opened again the fingerprint is recomputed and the record shows "unchanged since sign-off" or a warning if it no longer matches. The sign-off, the statement and the fingerprint are printed on the PDF and included in the CSV log (signed_by, signed_at, fingerprint), so an exported record can be checked against the device copy. A record that has not been signed in the app prints with a handwritten signature line instead. Signing is a click-to-accept declaration; it does not change the figures, and a signed record is listed with "signed" in the side panel.

Below the records list, Export log (CSV) shares a one-line-per-flight spreadsheet (date, route, PIC, crew and passenger counts and weights, fuel, weights and CG, W&B verdict, take-off and landing distances and speeds) and Export records (JSON) shares every record in full. The record carries a signature line for the pilot in command; the PDF or printout, signed, is the compliance copy. Records are stored only on the device until exported, so export regularly.

The calculation is only as good as the profile. Enter the empty weight and arm from the aircraft's own report, confirm template figures against the POH, and treat the result as an aid: the pilot in command remains responsible for the aircraft's weight and balance.

Times, fuel and logbook

Times, take-offs and landings with their night counts, the approach flown (visual, or ILS, LOC, RNAV/RNP, VOR or NDB, which the record classes as instrument), fuel uplift and fuel at shutdown are entered on the Plan tab's Enroute and Complete pages as the flight happens (each time field has a Now button and a clear button). Flight time (blocks) and air time are worked out and kept on the record when the flight is completed. Each record's Logbook row sends the entry straight to LogTen on the same device, or exports it in ForeFlight's template layout or as a generic CSV that MyFlightbook, Safelog and spreadsheets accept; the Flight records list exports the whole logbook the same ways. Each passenger also has DG (declared no undeclared dangerous goods) and Brief (safety briefing acknowledged) boxes, recorded on the manifest.

12c. Operator link, duty, currency and maintenance release

These tools live on the Status page under Pilot and operator.

  • NAIPS direct from the installed app. The iPad/Android app (built from the native folder of the repository) reaches NAIPS directly: enter your NAIPS username and password under Status › Operator link, press Test NAIPS login, and briefings, winds and flight plan filing work with no connector and no server. The same TAF/METAR/NOTAM decoding, briefing rules and fuel requirement run inside the app. NAIPS access can be set to Automatic (direct in the installed app, connector in a browser), Direct, or Always through the connector; an operator connector is still needed for the roster, fleet pack, notices and record upload.
  • Why a connector is needed for NAIPS in a browser. Airservices does not allow web pages to call NAIPS directly, so briefings, winds and flight plan filing always go through a connector: a small web service that forwards the request. Enter its address under Operator link first; without one, Test NAIPS login reports that no connector address is set. You do not need an operator to have one – the quickest way is the one-click link Deploy to Render (also shown in the Operator link sheet): sign in to Render, it reads the blueprint in the repository, asks for one value – AEROE_TOKENS, which you set to yourname: followed by a long random secret – and builds the service. When it shows Live, enter its onrender.com address as the Connector address, the secret as the Access token and yourname as the Pilot ID, press Test connection, then enter your NAIPS login and press Test NAIPS login. Do not put the NAIPS website itself in the Connector address field; the app will tell you if you do. The free plan sleeps after 15 minutes idle, so the first press after a break can take a minute. connector/README.md has the detail and other hosting options.
  • Your own NAIPS login. In Operator link you can enter your personal NAIPS username and password. Briefings, winds and flight plan filing then use your account instead of the operator's; the login stays on this device and goes to the connector with each request over HTTPS. Test NAIPS login checks it. The roster feed and fleet pack are only offered when the connector reports it has them, so a pilot or small operator can use the rest without a rostering system.
  • Operator link. An operator can run a small connector server (interface in docs/INTEGRATION.md) in front of its rostering and records systems. Enter its address, your token and pilot ID. Then: Test connection; Fetch today's roster and load a flight into the manifest with From roster on the W&B page; Fetch fleet pack to receive the operator's registrations, checklist customisations, standard weights and duty limit; Fetch notices to read company notices and send read receipts; and switch on automatic upload so every saved or signed flight record goes to the operator, queued until there is signal. Each record also has an Upload button.
  • Standard weights. When the operator has set them, a Std button beside each passenger fills the approved weight for an adult, child or infant and the record marks the weight with *.
  • Duty. Start duty when you sign on and End duty when you finish. The W&B page shows time on duty and, if a maximum duty period is set, the time remaining, in amber under an hour and red when exceeded. It is a reminder against your operator's fatigue rules, not a substitute for them.
  • Pilot currency. Enter medical, flight review, IPC, line check, DG and emergency procedures dates for expiry reminders; hours and landings in the last 30, 90 and 365 days are counted from the flight records on the device.
  • Maintenance release. In Edit profile, each registration can carry hours remaining and an expiry date. Saving a flight record deducts its block time and notes the before and after figures on the record; the W&B page warns when fewer than 5 hours or 7 days remain. Confirm against the aircraft's actual maintenance release.

12d. Plan: the flight sequence from briefing to logbook

The Plan tab runs a flight in order. A step bar at the top of each of its pages shows Plan & brief → W&B → Perf → Pre-flight sign-off → Enroute → Complete → Logbook, with each step's status; tap a step to go to it. Briefings and flight plan filing go through the operator connector, which holds the NAIPS account (Status › Operator link); everything else works stand-alone.

  • Find aerodrome. Type a code, name or town to search 2,680 Australian aerodromes and set the result as departure, destination, alternate or a waypoint (Via). The data is OurAirports' public-domain list: use it for planning geometry and codes, and ERSA for operational detail.
  • Legs and map. The legs table gives great-circle track and distance and the ETE from the TAS entered (nil wind); Use computed EET fills the elapsed times. A route diagram is drawn offline; Show map draws the route on OpenStreetMap when there is signal.
  • Import a plan from your EFB. Flight › Import plan takes an OzRunways plan (.ozpln: share the plan from OzRunways, save it to Files, then pick it here), a Garmin FPL file (AvPlan, ForeFlight and Garmin export one) or a GPX route. An OzRunways plan brings the airways with the LSALT of every segment, the winds OzRunways used for each leg, the cruise level, the ETD and the persons on board; the route reads as filed, for example JT W127 OTKUN W14 CAR, and the legs expand to every fix. The file's own coordinates are kept with the plan, so its waypoints resolve without a navigation database. Paste route takes a typed route string. Plan on whichever EFB you like, then let AeroE do the POH fuel plan, the briefing rules, the nav log and the records.
  • Alternates. The Flight section has Departure, Destination, Destination alternate 1 and 2, and an Enroute alternates field for any aerodromes you want briefed and assessed for a diversion along the way. Destination alternates are assessed for an arrival after the time to alternate; en route alternates for an arrival around the middle of the flight, with a wider time window.
  • Minima for this flight. Under the aerodrome fields, a table lists every aerodrome on the plan with its entered minima and an Edit button. The editor holds, per aerodrome, the alternate planning minima (ceiling and visibility), the take-off alternate minima (used when that aerodrome is the departure), and each instrument approach with its DA or MDA in feet AMSL and visibility. Heights: alternate and take-off alternate ceilings are feet above aerodrome level, as published and as the TAF and METAR report cloud; DA and MDA are feet AMSL as printed on the plate, and the briefing converts them to a height above the aerodrome using the built-in elevation. The entries are shared across flights (they are the same table as Status › Navigation data › Aerodrome minima).
  • What the briefing does with them. Alternate minima decide whether the destination requires an alternate and whether an INTER or TEMPO below them needs 30 or 60 minutes of holding fuel, and whether each alternate is suitable. Approach minima are compared with the forecast at ETA: a note when the forecast supports the approaches, a warning when it supports some, and an alternate requirement at the destination when it supports none; an INTER or TEMPO below the approach minima but above the alternate minima is flagged as likely holding. Take-off alternate minima are compared with the departure's current METAR (or TAF): below them, the verdict says a take-off alternate is required. Approach minima themselves still come from your licensed plates; the app does not hold them.
  • Charts. Area charts (NAIPS) beside the Briefing areas field opens the NAIPS chart directory: the Planning Chart Australia for the briefing area boundaries, plus GAF, GPWT and SIGWX charts. Filter the list, tap Show, and the chart appears; it is fetched with your NAIPS login and not stored.
  • Aerodrome minima. Status › Navigation data › Aerodrome minima holds each aerodrome's alternate minima (ceiling and visibility) and a note such as the approach it comes from, entered from your ERSA/DAP or Jeppesen documents, imported as a CSV (icao,ceiling,vis,note), or set by the operator's fleet pack. The briefing rules use the table entry for the destination and alternates; the plan's Alternate minima fields are the default for aerodromes not in the table. Instrument approach minima themselves are in licensed charts and are not automated.
  • Page layout. The Plan page is in collapsible sections; Fuel policy and Flight plan details start closed and the page remembers what you open. The action buttons and the briefing always stay visible.
  • Fuel plan. Built by segment from the type's POH profile: taxi, climb to the cruise level (time, fuel, distance), cruise at that level (TAS and fuel flow at maximum cruise or best range power), descent, alternate, holding from the briefing, variable and fixed reserves, against the fuel on board from the W&B tab. Every figure is editable; Recalculate from POH restores the POH values and Use this plan's EET and flow carries the totals into the briefing and the flight plan. The C208 profile comes from POH Figures 5-18, 5-19 and 5-25 (cargo pod, 8,750 lb, ISA); the GA8 profile from the AFM climb rates and best-economy cruise table, with assumed climb and descent fuel flows that you should replace with your operator's figures.
  • Map. Show map (online) opens an interactive map under the legs. The layers button (top right) switches between Street, Terrain and Satellite hybrid base maps; with a free openAIP key entered under Status › Operator link › Map overlays, an Aeronautical overlay adds airspace, aerodromes and navaids (close in content to an ERC-Lo; openAIP's data licence is non-commercial, so an operator should check it or use its own chart subscription). Airservices' WAC and ERC charts are licensed products and are not included. Tap any aerodrome to set it as departure, destination, via point or alternate; tap a leg to insert a turning point at that spot; tap empty map to add a turning point at the end of the route; tap a via point or alternate to remove it. Turning points are written into the route as ICAO lat/long (3340S15110E) and go into the flight plan that way. The route diagram above the map works offline; tiles need signal.
  • Rain radar. The Radar button at the bottom left of the map (Plan and Enroute pages) overlays the last two hours of the Bureau of Meteorology radar composite, via RainViewer, in ten-minute frames with step and play buttons. It needs signal; the latest frame is marked.
  • Prohibited, restricted and danger areas. With airspace data loaded (Status › Navigation data), the planner lists every P, R and D area the track crosses, with the distance along the leg, the area's limits, and whether the planned level is within, above or below it (the first and last legs count climb and descent). A prohibited area on track is a red warning; restricted and danger areas say to check activation. The map draws the areas and tapping one shows its limits. Sources: the Airservices Airspace Data product (Dataset 14 SUA CSV, the official licensed boundaries), an openAIP download with your key (community data, non-commercial licence), or a GeoJSON file. Activation times are not in any of these: the briefing NOTAMs and the DAH hold them, and the table notes when an area is mentioned in the briefing.
  • IFR routes, waypoints and LSALT. With navigation data loaded (Status › Navigation data: the Airservices General Aviation Data CSV files for IFR and VFR waypoints, navaids and IFR routes with LSALT), the Route field accepts published waypoints, navaids and ATS route designators, for example BTH H65 CUL. The planner expands each airway between the two named points, shows the LSALT per leg and warns when the planned level is below it; DCT legs have no published LSALT, so use the grid LSALT. Airways go into the flight plan as ATS route segments. The map draws the routes and waypoints (zoom in); tap a segment to add it to the route, tap a waypoint to add it as a via. Nothing is bundled with the app because the AIP's conditions of use do not allow it; the Airservices data is a paid subscription. With a free openAIP key, Status › Navigation data can load Australia's navaids and reporting points (community data, non-commercial licence), so fixes such as a VOR identifier resolve; airways are not in openAIP, so either type the fixes along the airway or import the Airservices routes file.
  • Route suggestions. Under the legs the planner lists routes you have briefed or filed before for the same city pair, with how often, and, through the connector, the operator's preferred routes for the pair. Tap one to use it.
  • Your own charts. Under Status › Operator link › Custom chart sources you can add up to three tile or WMS sources for charts you or your operator hold a licence for (Airservices VFR and IFR charts are not included in the app). They appear in the map's layers button as base maps or overlays, and an operator's fleet pack can set them for every pilot.
  • Equipment codes. Beside Equipment (10a), Surveillance (10b) and PBN, Choose… opens a list of every ICAO code with its meaning; tick what is fitted and the field is written in the right order. A line under the fields warns of mismatches, such as PBN codes without R in 10a or GNSS PBN codes without G.
  • Winds. Enter a wind per leg, or Load winds (NAIPS) to fetch the wind and temperature profile for the route; the wind nearest the cruise level is applied to every leg and can then be edited leg by leg. Ground speed, leg times and trip fuel follow.
  • Navigation log (Enroute page). Every waypoint of the route, including the points an airway expands to, with cumulative distance, leg time, planned ETA and planned fuel on board. Leg times and fuel come from the fuel plan (winds, climb, cruise flow, descent), scaled so the legs add up to the trip. ETAs run from the ETD until the airborne time is entered, then from that. At each waypoint enter the actual time (or tap now) and the fuel on board: the remaining ETAs are revised from the last actual time, the fuel column shows the difference from plan, and a line projects the fuel at destination from the last reading and the planned burn to run, in red when it would land below the fixed reserve. The alternate leg is shown underneath. The log goes into the flight record and its PDF at completion.
  • Pre-flight sign-off. Save pre-flight record and sign takes the manifest, W&B, performance and plan as they stand and opens the record for the pre-flight acceptance. Times and landings are not part of it, because they are not known yet.
  • Enroute. Off-blocks and take-off times (Now buttons), airborne time, ETA and SARTIME, the legs with planned fuel per leg, and fuel checks: enter the fuel now and position, and the table shows the planned figure, the actual burn rate so far and the projected fuel at destination, red if it would land below the fixed reserve.
  • Complete. Landing and on-blocks times, take-offs and landings with night counts, the approach flown, fuel uplift and fuel at shutdown. Complete flight copies them onto the record, deducts the block time from the maintenance release and opens the record for the completion acceptance, which is a second click-to-accept with its own fingerprint. The logbook exports then appear on the same page, and Start next flight clears the flight for the next sector while keeping the completed record.
  • Route and times. Departure, destination, up to two alternates, ETD (local time, sent to NAIPS as UTC), estimated elapsed time, time to the alternate, cruise level, TAS, route waypoints and optional briefing areas.
  • Fuel policy and limits. Fuel flow, fixed and variable reserves, taxi fuel, the alternate minima to apply (1,500 ft and 8 km unless the aerodrome publishes lower), the aircraft's crosswind and tailwind limits and the destination runway heading. Fuel on board comes from the registration selected in the W&B tab.
  • Get NAIPS briefing. Fetches TAF, METAR/SPECI, TTF, NOTAM and SIGMET for the aerodromes, then shows: a verdict line (alternate required, holding minutes, which alternates are suitable), the findings for each aerodrome with the quoted product and the rule applied, the fuel plan against fuel on board, an optional draft summary, and the raw products to read. No TAF, a TAF not covering ETA ± 30 min, cloud more than SCT or visibility below the minima, thunderstorms or winds beyond the limits require an alternate; TEMPO below minima carries 60 min holding, INTER 30 min, a bare PROB 60 min; relevant NOTAMs (closures, lighting, navaids, fuel, obstacles) are flagged. These rules are planning aids that your operator must align with its exposition and the AIP.
  • Flight plan. The ICAO fields are filled from the aircraft type, the registration, the manifest count and your pilot name and phone (Status › Pilot). Preview plan shows the FPL text; File with NAIPS (two taps) lodges it and shows NAIPS's response. Changes after filing are made through NAIPS or the briefing office.
  • Record. The briefing verdict, findings, fuel plan, summary and the filed plan text are attached to the next flight record saved from the W&B page, so the signed record carries the planning basis.
The briefing is as good as the products and the rules it applies. The pilot in command must read the raw products, apply the operator's rules and the AIP, and decide whether the flight can proceed.

12b. Performance: take-off and landing distances

The Perf tab reads the POH take-off and landing charts for the conditions you enter. It has two pages, Take-off distance and Landing distance. Enter the aerodrome elevation and QNH (the app works out pressure height at 30 ft per hectopascal), the temperature, the runway heading and the wind as broadcast (ATIS and AWIS give magnetic directions, a METAR gives true), the runway slope and surface, and the weight. From W&B copies the take-off or landing weight of the registration currently selected in the W&B tab. Leave the runway heading blank to enter the wind directly as a headwind component (negative for a tailwind).

  • Results. Pressure height; head or tail and crosswind components; the take-off safety speed or reference approach speed for the weight (and, for a power-off approach, the rate of descent); the distance to or from 50 ft and the ground roll, both unfactored as the POH prints them and multiplied by your safety factor; and, if you enter the runway length available, a RUNWAY SUFFICIENT or INSUFFICIENT verdict on the factored distance. The chart path line lists the distance after each panel so you can check the reading on the paper chart.
  • Wind rule. By default the app uses half the headwind and one and a half times the tailwind, the usual allowance when a chart has none. Switch it off to read the chart exactly as printed.
  • Limits. The app refuses to extrapolate: above the chart's highest temperature or pressure height, with more than the chart's tailwind, outside its slope range or over its maximum weight it gives no result and says why. Below the lowest scheduled temperature or pressure height it uses the lowest scheduled figure, as the AFM directs. A crosswind above the maximum demonstrated is flagged.
  • Checking. Load AFM example fills the chart's own worked example so you can compare the app with the manual. Charts are digitised from the POH drawing and remain marked unverified until a pilot has worked several cases on the printed chart and compared them.

Charts are entered per type. The GA8 has its three GA-FM-04 charts (take-off 14° flap, 3° approach landing, power-off landing). The C208B has the POH short field take-off and landing tables for both configurations, cargo pod installed and without pod, chosen with the Configuration selector; the POH gives no slope correction for the Caravan, so slope is shown but not applied, and above 40 °C the take-off figures are increased by 20 % up to the ISA + 35 °C operating limit. Other types show the tab with a note until their POH data is added.

Performance output is an aid. The pilot in command must confirm take-off and landing performance against the AFM, apply the factors their operation requires, and allow for conditions the charts do not cover such as soft, wet or long-grass surfaces.

13. Voice: read aloud, spoken replies and recall

Voice is off until you turn it on under Status → Voice. It has three parts.

Read aloud

With Read aloud on, opening any checklist shows a voice bar at the bottom with a Start button. The app speaks each line, challenge then response, highlights it, and waits. Tap ✓ Checked (or the line itself) to tick it and hear the next. Repeat says it again, Skip moves on without ticking, Stop pauses. Notes are read and passed over; at a branch the app reads the question and the options and waits for you to choose. In normal checklists it reads the short form by default: the challenge and the capitalised action, for example “Fuel shutoff, ON”, leaving bracketed detail on the screen; abnormal and emergency procedures are always read in full, and the short form can be switched off under Status → Voice. At the end it says the checklist is complete. Speaking rate and voice are set on the Status page, and Test the voice lets you hear them. Read aloud works offline and plays through a paired Bluetooth headset.

Voice replies (experimental)

With Voice replies on, the app listens for a few seconds after each line. Say “checked”, or the response as written in capitals (for example “both on” or “idle cutoff”), to tick and continue; “repeat”, “skip” or “stop”. At a branch, say the first word of the option, for example “hot” or “no”. The bar shows a red dot while listening and prints what it heard, so a wrong hearing is visible before it matters; a tick can always be undone by tapping the line. The first use asks for microphone permission. On iPad and iPhone the recognition is done by Apple and needs signal; Android can work offline. A paired Bluetooth headset supplies the microphone, which keeps engine noise out. If the app cannot hear you it simply waits for a tap.

Say procedure

With Voice replies on, a Say procedure button appears above the checklist list. Tap it and say the name, for example “landing checklist”, “engine failure in flight” or “low oil pressure”. The app opens the best match for the current aircraft and, if Read aloud is on, starts reading it.

“Hey Aero” wake word

Turn on “Hey Aero” wake word under Status → Voice and the app keeps the microphone open while it is on screen. Say “hey Aero, landing checklist”, “hey Aero, before takeoff”, “hey Aero, engine failure in flight”, “hey Aero, approach checklist”, “hey Aero, status” or “hey Aero, stop”. A single tone acknowledges the wake word if you pause after it; two tones confirm the command. A red dot beside the aircraft name shows the app is listening. It only works while the app is in front: an installed web app cannot listen in the background or with the screen locked. It uses more battery and, on iPad and iPhone, needs signal.

Editing normal checklists

Every normal checklist can be tailored to an operator's requirements. Open it and tap Edit at the top right: add, change, remove or reorder lines, add a note, or use Reload original lines to start again. Branch questions such as “Engine condition?” stay as a block that can be moved or deleted but not edited inside. Write the action in capitals so the voice reads it in short form. Save, and the list shows ✎ beside the name and “Customised on this device” under the title; Restore original removes all edits. Abnormal and emergency procedures cannot be edited.

Edits are kept on this device, per aircraft. To give a fleet the same checklists, make the edits on one device, use Export customisations on the Status page and send the text to each pilot, who opens the same aircraft and uses Import to paste it. Importing replaces that device's edits for that aircraft.

Custom approach checklist

Each aircraft can also have one checklist of your own. On the Status page, under Customised checklists, tap Create next to the approach entry to start from a template or a blank list. Save, and it appears under Normal between Descent and Before Landing. It is included in Export and Import.

One approach checklist for all favourites. Turn this switch on, in the same section, and every aircraft you have marked ★ uses a single shared approach checklist; editing it from any favourite changes it for all of them. Aircraft that are not favourites keep their own. If the aircraft you are on already had its own approach checklist when you turn the switch on, that list becomes the shared one.

Voice settings on the Status page

  • Read aloud, Short responses in normal checklists (challenge plus the capitalised action only; abnormal and emergency are always read in full), “Hey Aero” wake word, Cue tones (one tone when listening starts, a soft tick when a word is accepted), and Voice replies.
  • Open microphone early appears on Android only. On iPad, iPhone and Mac speech and listening cannot overlap, so the microphone opens as soon as a line has been read: wait for the single tone, then reply.
  • Speaking rate in five steps, each played as a sample when tapped, and a choice of Voice from those on the device.
  • Test the voice speaks a sample line. Test the microphone listens once and prints exactly what the device reported, including any error code.
  • Under the wake word switch a status line shows whether the wake listener is running and the last words it heard. The Voice log at the end records recent voice events with times, for troubleshooting.
  • Replies are accepted as “checked” or the written response itself (“both on”, “idle cutoff”), plus “repeat”, “skip” and “stop”. A phrase containing “hey Aero” is never treated as a reply.

Offline recognition (experimental)

By default, spoken replies, Say procedure and the wake word use the device's own recogniser, which on iPad, iPhone and Mac sends the audio to Apple and therefore needs signal. Offline recognition, under Status → Voice, replaces it with an engine that runs inside the app. Turning it on downloads about 46 MB once, with a progress bar; the download is kept separately from the app, survives updates, and can be removed with Remove the offline download. Once it shows Ready, replies, Say procedure and “hey Aero” all work with no signal and no audio leaves the device.

The offline engine only listens for the words the app expects: the reply words, the capitalised responses of the current aircraft, branch options, the checklist and procedure names, and the wake phrases. That restriction is what makes it accurate in engine noise, and it also means it cannot understand anything else. There is no need to record your voice: the engine is trained on many thousands of speakers and does not adapt to one. Use Test the microphone to see what it hears when you say each command, and the voice log if something seems off.

With the offline engine the microphone stays open between lines, so a reply is heard the moment the line ends. If, on your device, the voice is muted or quiet while the offline engine is on, turn on Release microphone while speaking; the app then closes the microphone during each line and reopens it afterwards, at the cost of a short pause. The first time, the browser asks for microphone permission.

Voice is an aid to the displayed checklist, not a replacement for it. Read the screen, and treat a spoken tick like any other tick.

14. If something looks wrong

  • A change you were told about is not showing. Check the Version row in the App section of the Status page. If it is older than expected, wait ten minutes from publication, tap "Check for updates now", close the app fully and reopen it. The green bar on the first launch after an update names the version and has a What's new button.
  • A voice-opened checklist is not read aloud. Tap anywhere in the app once after launching it; Apple requires a tap before the app may speak. Then check the Voice log for “speech started” after each “speak:” line.
  • The wake word opens the wrong checklist. Say the full name and pause briefly after it. The voice log shows what was heard and what was opened.
  • There is no Version row. The copy on the device is from before updates were tracked, usually one installed from the old address. Delete the icon and install again from the address in section 1. Progress and favourites carry over.
  • Progress disappeared. Browser site data was cleared, or the app was opened in a private window. Saved data lives only in the device's browser storage.
  • Voice replies do nothing. Check that the microphone was allowed for the app (Settings → the browser or the installed app → Microphone), that the device has signal on iPad or iPhone unless Offline recognition shows Ready, and that Voice replies is on under Status → Voice. If the device still cannot hear you, Read aloud with a tap to confirm continues to work.
  • Offline recognition will not download or load. It needs signal for the one-off download and about 50 MB of free storage. Private browsing does not keep the download. If it shows a problem, tap Try again, or Remove the offline download and start over.
  • A figure or procedure looks wrong. It may well be. The repository's review list records which figures the authors were least sure of for each aircraft, and corrections are made in the data file for that type. Report it with the aircraft and the checklist line.
AeroE Checklist is an open prototype. Content and software are provided as a starting point for an operator's own approved checklist, not as a substitute for one.
